Software

Both projects below are temporally evolving knowledge graphs of a research literature. A link between two entities is not a fact the graph states once. It carries the year by year count of papers reporting it, the assertions extracted from those papers, and the sentences those came from, so a connection can be dated to its first appearance, watched as it strengthens or fades, and measured against the growth of the field rather than read as a fixed fact.

The same pipeline has been applied to two literatures so far. Each graph is built from its own PubMed corpus, annotated with PubTator 3.0, and queried in the browser over the published tables. The two are separate graphs and separate corpora, and neither contains the other.

  • Temporally Evolving GLP-1 Knowledge Graph

    2026 · in development

    A temporally resolved knowledge graph of the GLP-1 literature, the hormone and the drugs that act on its receptor, built from 41,234 PubMed records spanning 1978 to 2026, which is as far back as this literature goes. The corpus has doubled since 2022, so most of this field is recent, and the graph dates each connection rather than flattening it. The tagger it is built on does not know the drugs this field now turns on, so semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide and four others are matched by name against the same text and marked as such: their links are co-mentions, and they carry none of the extracted assertions the rest of the graph does. Opening one says so before it says anything else.

  • Temporally Evolving T1D Knowledge Graph

    2026 · in development

    A temporally resolved knowledge graph of the type 1 diabetes literature, built from 131,511 PubMed records spanning 1960 to 2026. Every link between two entities retains its year by year paper counts, the assertions extracted from the text, and the sentences supporting it, so a connection can be dated to its first appearance and measured against a corpus baseline rather than read as a static fact. The interface provides entity and pair timelines, burst detection, shortest paths between entities never studied together, and retrieval of the underlying passages.
